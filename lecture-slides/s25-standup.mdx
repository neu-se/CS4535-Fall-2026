## How Is Your Team Meeting?

<div style={{fontSize: '0.8em', textAlign: 'left'}}>

One person per team, about a minute each:

1. When were all of you last in one place, in person?
2. Out of class, do you meet in person, on a call, or only in text?
3. Where do your decisions get written down?

</div>

<aside className="notes">
Five minutes. Go team by team. No judgment on the answers. The point is that each team hears how the others work.

> **Transition:** You already said why this matters, last week.
</aside>

## Nov 23: A Realistic Goal

<div style={{fontSize: '0.75em', textAlign: 'left'}}>

Feature freeze is in two weeks. At your table, work it out in this order:

1. **Look back.** What has each of you shipped since September, and what did you have to learn to do it? A large codebase you'd never seen, its database and deploys, review, your users
2. **Look ahead.** What's left between today and a user doing your main flow on staging, behind your flag?
3. **Pick the goal.** What can this team actually finish in two weeks, at the pace you've really been working? Most of the learning is behind you now. Cut the rest, and say so

Post one paragraph: **what a user can do on Nov 23 that they can't do today**. Then move everything else off the Nov 23 milestone.

</div>

<aside className="notes">
Nine of thirteen first tickets have merged. Every one of those took learning the codebase, its migrations and its CI from zero. That's the slow part, and it's mostly done. Push teams to plan from what they've actually delivered per week, not from the September plan.

The paragraph replaces charter section 2's Nov 23 statement where the two disagree.

> **Transition:** And the habits that get you there.
</aside>

</Slide>

## This Week's Ask

<div style={{fontSize: '0.72em', textAlign: 'left'}}>

1. **One in-person slot a week**, written in your charter. Class time counts
2. **Notes from every meeting** in `notes/meetings/YYYY-MM-DD.md`, within a day: who came, what you decided, who owns each next step. AI-written notes are fine. One named person checks them before they're posted. Recording needs everyone's OK
3. **At every meeting, go through the open PRs** that touch a migration, a shared table or a shared file, and who has told whom
4. **Charter section 9: "How we use agents."** Draft it today, finalize it after Nov 16
5. **If a review concern isn't settled in one round of writing, talk.** 15 minutes, in person or on a call. The author shows the change follows the conventions, then posts a short summary on the thread

</div>

<aside className="notes">
Frame these as how you'll hit the Nov 23 goal, not as new rules for their own sake.

Item 2: Massachusetts requires every party's consent to record a conversation, and live transcription counts.

Item 5 is a mentorship moment either way: if the author can show it, the reviewer learns how; if they can't, the author learns what was missing.

## The Post: by 10:30, in your team's channel

<div style={{fontSize: '0.75em', textAlign: 'left'}}>

- **Done:** what you finished since the last standup, with links
- **Blocked:** what's in your way, and *the specific thing you need from someone*. "Nothing" is fine if it's true
- **Next:** what you'll do before the next standup
- **This week:** link the board issues you'll close before Nov 23

The post is your attendance. "Still working on it" doesn't meet the format: A teammate who reads it should know what you did and what you need.

</div>

<aside className="notes">
Same three questions as the async sprint. Read the posts against each team's board. An issue that's "next" in every standup since Oct 26 is a cut to discuss at the table.

> **Transition:** Then the tables.
</aside>
