## Issue to Reviewed PR

<div style={{fontSize: '0.6em', textAlign: 'left'}}>

<div style={{backgroundColor: 'rgba(42,157,143,0.12)', padding: '0.6em', borderRadius: '8px', marginBottom: '0.5em', textAlign: 'center', fontSize: '1.2em'}}>

**Issue**, then **goal**, then the agent works, then **review**, then **merge**

</div>

The teams behind Cursor, Devin, Claude Code, Codex, and PostHog are all working toward automating this path. The tools will keep changing. The workflow is the same in all of them. Someone states the goal, an agent does the work, and a person reviews the result and answers for it.

<div style={{backgroundColor: 'rgba(100,150,255,0.14)', padding: '0.5em', borderRadius: '8px', marginTop: '0.5em'}}>

**Where the platform goes next.** Once the bug reporter in #1050 merges, an agent running in a sandbox can watch Sentry for bug reports and open fixes on its own. Every one of those fixes still needs a person to review it before it merges.

</div>

</div>

<aside className="notes">
**The more of this path is automated, the more the review step matters.** When an agent opens fixes on its own, the person who approves the merge is the one who answers for it.

## What You Can Judge

<Img src="/img/agents-activity/judgement-curve.svg" alt="As distance from your expertise grows, what you can produce with AI stays high. What you can produce without AI falls off first, then your judgement of code, and your judgement of the product much later. Above the product judgement curve is work you can make but can't judge." style={{width: '82%'}} />

<aside className="notes">
**Read the graph left to right.** Near your own code, you can judge what you make. Moving away from your expertise, what you can produce with AI barely drops, but what you can judge does.

**The orange zone is the problem.** You can make it, but you can't judge it, so someone else has to: your reviewers and maintainers. #1050's review bot giving up at 291 files is what that looks like. Expectations also rise to absorb the extra output.

**There are two judgement curves, not one.** That's the hopeful part, and it's the next slide.

> **Transition:** Your product judgement reaches much farther than your code judgement.
</aside>

</Slide>

## Stay Within What You Can Judge

<div style={{fontSize: '0.56em', textAlign: 'left'}}>

<div style={{display: 'flex', gap: '0.8em'}}>

<div style={{flex: 1, backgroundColor: 'rgba(27,175,122,0.13)', padding: '0.5em', borderRadius: '8px'}}>

**Product judgement reaches far.** You're a user. You can see a user ID where a name belongs, or a tab that forgets where you were, without reading any code. Your team's `user-research.md` says whose jobs matter. So the agent's analysis can range widely.

</div>

<div style={{flex: 1, backgroundColor: 'rgba(42,120,214,0.13)', padding: '0.5em', borderRadius: '8px'}}>

**Code judgement falls off sooner.** In a subsystem you've never read, you can't tell a correct change from a plausible one. So the PR has to be small enough to read line by line.

</div>

</div>

<div style={{backgroundColor: 'rgba(231,111,81,0.15)', padding: '0.5em', borderRadius: '8px', marginTop: '0.5em'}}>

**Don't make other people pay for your AI use.** If you hand people AI-generated text you haven't read, they do the reading for you. Read Christian Kästner's ["Stop externalizing the cost of your AI use to me"](https://thelastsoftwareengineer.substack.com/p/stop-externalizing-the-cost-of-your) before you post anything an agent wrote.

</div>

</div>

<aside className="notes">
**Two things follow for review.** "The tests pass" is pass-or-fail. Nobody has good measures yet for whether a change is minimal, keeps the code's design intact, or is easy to extend. Those are what your review checks, and a `/goal` evaluator doesn't.

**And reviewers need layers.** "Please read this summary of my changes" is already too much. A short summary, the parts that matter to each audience, and underneath, the code and the agent's transcript. The grading interface report later in this deck is close to that.

> **Transition:** What everyone does today.
</aside>

## Today: The Six-Step Workflow

<div style={{fontSize: '0.5em', textAlign: 'left'}}>

Everyone does the same activity, on a different part of the platform, using the [six-step workflow from CS 3100](https://neu-pdi.github.io/CS3100-Spring-2026/lecture-slides/l13-ai-coding-assistants) (Kam et al.).

| Step | What you do | When |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Identify** | Pick a flow from your team's `user-research.md`, or an XL pool ticket. Claim it in the thread | Class, 5 min |
| **2. Engage** | The agent analyzes the flow and takes screenshots. You walk the same flow by hand | Class, ~25 min |
| **3. Evaluate** | Compare its report with your notes. Mark each finding evidence or guess | Class, 10 min |
| **4. Calibrate** | Turn what holds up into an XL ticket, scoped into PRs you can review | When you start your ticket |
| **5. Tweak** | `/goal` builds the first PR and leaves a demo running. You click through it | When you start your ticket |
| **6. Finalize** | `/code-review` your own PR and write its description | When you start your ticket |

</div>

<p style={{fontSize: '0.55em', textAlign: 'left', marginTop: '0.5em'}}>
<strong>Class time is steps 1 to 3.</strong> Steps 4 to 6 aren't due this week. They're where your project work and your First Implementation Ticket start.
</p>

<aside className="notes">
**The six steps are Identify, Engage, Evaluate, Calibrate, Tweak, Finalize,** from Kam et al.'s study of how expert developers work with AI, which CS 3100 taught in L13 and Lab 6.

**Steps 4 to 6 feed the First Implementation Ticket,** assigned Oct 15 and merged by Oct 29. Pick by Mon Oct 19. An own-project ticket needs its XL scope agreed with Jon on the issue by then.

> **Transition:** Setup, which should already be done.

## "Dangerously" Skip Permissions

<div style={{fontSize: '0.52em', textAlign: 'left'}}>

<div style={{backgroundColor: 'rgba(231,111,81,0.15)', padding: '0.5em', borderRadius: '8px', marginBottom: '0.4em'}}>

**The flag runs every command without asking.** Your workspace is an isolated container you can rebuild, so the damage stays inside it. Don't use the flag on your laptop. **Your GitHub login leaves the container:** the agent can push, force-push, and comment on the public repo as you.

</div>

<div style={{backgroundColor: 'rgba(42,157,143,0.12)', padding: '0.5em', borderRadius: '8px', marginBottom: '0.4em'}}>

**Unsure? Use auto mode.** Shift+Tab until the status line shows it. Routine actions run without asking, so `/goal` still works unattended, and a classifier blocks risky ones such as a force-push.

</div>

<div style={{backgroundColor: 'rgba(100,150,255,0.14)', padding: '0.5em', borderRadius: '8px'}}>

**Agents can go further than anyone planned.** In a July 2026 evaluation, over a thousand agents coordinated, tampered with their transcripts, and broke into Hugging Face's production systems. One [analysis](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/HsijShdRdAg5sPKnF/an-unexamined-cause-of-the-openai-hugging-face-hacking) blames a pass-or-fail score: "Once an agent's transcript guarantees failure, further misconduct *cannot make its score any worse*."

</div>

</div>

<aside className="notes">
**The connection to this activity:** a `/goal` condition is pass-or-fail too. An agent that can't make the tests pass honestly might weaken the tests instead. That's one more reason to read the diff.

**The analysis is by Knox, Booth, and Christian.**

> **Transition:** Step 1.
</aside>

</Slide>

## Step 2: Engage

<div style={{fontSize: '0.5em', textAlign: 'left'}}>

<div style={{backgroundColor: 'rgba(120,120,120,0.10)', padding: '0.5em', borderRadius: '8px', marginBottom: '0.4em', fontFamily: 'monospace'}}>

Analyze &lt;the flow&gt; in Pawtograder, from the point of view of &lt;roles&gt;. Use the seeded class and take screenshots of the key steps; save them in ~/analysis/. We need to improve usability, and fix at least this bug: &lt;the bug&gt;. Consider all the variants of the flow: &lt;variants&gt;. &lt;What makes the flow painful&gt;. Recommend changes, most important first. Don't change any code yet: we'll discuss the ticket scope for a PR first.

</div>

For a pool ticket, start with "Read issue #N and reproduce it."

<div style={{backgroundColor: 'rgba(255,180,0,0.15)', padding: '0.5em', borderRadius: '8px', marginTop: '0.4em'}}>

**It takes about 25 minutes. Don't wait for it.** Walk the same flow yourself, as each role, and write down what's slow, confusing, or broken **before you read anything the agent wrote**. Keep the list.

</div>

</div>

<aside className="notes">
**"Don't change any code yet" matters.** It makes the agent stop at a proposal, so step 4 can happen.

**The student's own list is the point of the waiting time.** The reflection compares it with the agent's report, and that only works if the list was written first.

## Step 3: Evaluate

<div style={{fontSize: '0.5em', textAlign: 'left'}}>

**Check each finding against the app, not the agent's description of it.** Then mark it **evidence** (you can see it) or **guess** (what people want or feel).

| The report says | Check it by driving the app locally |
|---|---|
| The box shows a UUID | Log in as the grader. One click |
| The two Complete buttons write different tables | Click each, then look in Supabase Studio. It came from reading code |
| "Next" follows submission ID | With six seeded students, can this data even show the difference? |
| A grader can complete someone else's review | Bug or feature? The agent can't know. (Feature: graders split rubric parts) |
| The Survey tab is "the screen the grader actually wants" | A guess about graders. Ask a TA |

</div>

<p style={{fontSize: '0.5em', textAlign: 'left', marginTop: '0.4em'}}>
Also ask what it didn't cover: keyboard only, a screen reader, a class of 200.
</p>

<aside className="notes">
**The agent isn't a user,** not even one kind of user, the way the students are. Guesses go to the team's user study, not straight into code.

**This is where participation ends.** Students who stop here write up their reflection.

## Steps 5 and 6: Tweak and Finalize

<div style={{fontSize: '0.5em', textAlign: 'left'}}>

<div style={{backgroundColor: 'rgba(120,120,120,0.10)', padding: '0.5em', borderRadius: '8px', marginBottom: '0.4em', fontFamily: 'monospace'}}>

**Tweak:** open the demo through your port forward and click through it as each role. When something's off, tell Claude what to change, or edit the code yourself.

**Finalize:** open a draft PR, then:

1. `/code-review` your own PR, scoped to code you can judge
2. Decide which findings are real: reproduce it, point to the line, or throw it out
3. Write the description yourself, including what you still can't vouch for

</div>

| `claude --resume` | Session gone? Pick up a past conversation |

</div>

<div style={{flex: 1, backgroundColor: 'rgba(42,157,143,0.12)', padding: '0.5em', borderRadius: '8px'}}>

**Use the five-hour limit to your advantage.** Your usage limit resets every five hours, and the time you're away is free agent time. Before you stop for the day, start a big `/goal` in `tmux`. When you come back, you have a fresh limit and, with luck, finished work. Spend your time on reviewing it.

</div>
